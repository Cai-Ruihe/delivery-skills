# Research context and evaluation

Maintenance only. [Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657) studies failure modes in multi-agent systems. This citation provides context; it is not a benchmark of this skill snapshot or the configured models. The paper is not distributed in this repository.

## Evaluation

Use comparable tasks, acceptance bars and budgets. When comparison is useful, compare the primary alone, plus one worker, and plus a small admitted set. Include small standalone projects and assigned delivery work. Do not require a benchmark for every narrow correction.

Record the first real integrated check, agreed-outcome to verified-outcome time, failed substantive attempts, escaped defects, decision waits, repeated questions and actual usage/cost including coordination, review and recovery. Include unfinished, failed and stopped cases; distinguish model unavailability from reasoning errors.

Do not infer efficiency from version counts, subchat counts, worker prices or unadjusted cumulative counters. One successful pilot proves feasibility, not universal superiority. Revisit bounds and model allocation when task structure, platform capability or comparable outcome evidence changes. Actual net benefit remains Unknown until measured.
